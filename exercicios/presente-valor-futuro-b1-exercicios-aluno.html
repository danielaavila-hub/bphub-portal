Eu Faço
Depois…

"I eat later." In English that sentence is a bit odd. In Portuguese, it's exactly how people talk about the future all day long — no "will," no "going to." Let's find out why.

Presente do indicativo com valor de futuro Registro falado, informal
📘

Language Reference

Present tense, future meaning

📌 1. The pattern

In everyday spoken Brazilian Portuguese, the presente do indicativo is very often used to talk about the future — not the present. What makes this possible is a time expression that tells you the action isn't happening right now.

Eu como depois. — I'll eat later. (not "I eat later" as a habit)

Structure: presente do indicativo + expressão de tempo futuro (depois, mais tarde, amanhã, semana que vem…)

📌 2. Why this makes sense to an English speaker

English already does something similar — just in a narrower way. You use the simple present or present continuous for scheduled, fixed, or certain future events: "The train leaves at 9." "I'm meeting her tomorrow."

Portuguese takes this same logic and stretches it much further. It's not limited to schedules — it covers spontaneous decisions, promises, and casual plans too. "Eu como depois" isn't a schedule, it's just a decision made a minute ago — and the present tense works fine for it.

📌 3. The adverb is what shifts the meaning

Without a future-time expression, the same sentence would default to a habitual or immediate reading. Compare:

SentenceMeaning
Eu como às 8h. (no future adverb)I eat at 8. — habit
Eu como depois. (future adverb)I'll eat later. — future
Eu falo com ela. (no future adverb)I talk to her. — habit, or right now
Eu falo com ela depois da aula.I'll talk to her after class. — future
📌 4. Common future-time expressions
depois mais tarde daqui a pouco amanhã semana que vem mês que vem ano que vem na sexta / na segunda daqui a uma hora
Three ways to talk about the future in Portuguese
StrategyExampleRegister
Futuro do presenteEu falarei com ela depois.Formal, written — rare in speech
Ir + infinitivoEu vou falar com ela depois.Neutral — the "textbook future"
Presente + advérbioEu falo com ela depois.Very informal — extremely common in speech
Cultural note — a national pattern: this construction is at the heart of Brazil's famous "procrastination culture" — "Amanhã eu começo a dieta," "Segunda eu paro de fumar," "Ano que vem eu vou pra academia." These promises-for-later almost always use the present tense, not vou começar or começarei. Recognizing the pattern will help you understand a huge amount of casual Brazilian speech — and maybe laugh at the joke when a Brazilian friend makes one of these promises with a wink.

Complete a conversa

Fill in each blank using presente do indicativo + the adverb in brackets

Conjugate the verb in brackets in the presente do indicativo. Remember: the adverb is already doing the work of pointing to the future.

  • 1.— Você paga a conta agora?
    — Não, eu    (pagar)depois.
  • 2.— Vamos ao médico hoje?
    — A gente    (ir)semana que vem.
  • 3.— Me manda a resposta agora?
    — Eu te    (mandar)daqui a pouco.
  • 4.— Ela já sabe da novidade?
    — Ainda não, eu    (contar)pra ela amanhã.
  • 5.— Vocês entregam o projeto hoje?
    — Não, nós    (entregar)na sexta.

Sua conversa de WhatsApp

Write a short exchange postponing 3 things

Write a short WhatsApp-style exchange (6-8 lines) with a colleague or friend, in which you postpone at least three different things. Use presente do indicativo + a future-time adverb for each one — not "vou + infinitivo."

Modelo

— E o pagamento do fornecedor?

— Combinado. Ah, e nosso café?
— Semana que vem a gente marca, com certeza!

  • What three things are you postponing?
  • What future-time adverb fits each one (depois, amanhã, semana que vem, na sexta…)?
  • Does your conversation sound natural — like a real exchange between colleagues or friends?
