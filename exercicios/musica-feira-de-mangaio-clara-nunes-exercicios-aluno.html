Feira de Mangaio

Clara Nunes · from the album Esperança, 1979 · composed by Sivuca and Glorinha Gadelha

Few Northeastern Brazilian songs describe an open-air market in as much detail as this one. Curiously, it wasn't written in the Northeast: Sivuca and his wife, Glorinha Gadelha, created the song while living in New York — the lyrics were finished inside a McDonald's on Seventh Avenue. First recorded by Sivuca himself, the song became a classic in Clara Nunes's voice.

Cultural context

🗽 A Northeastern song written in New York
Sivuca (Severino Dias de Oliveira) was one of the greatest instrumentalists and composers of Northeastern Brazilian music, with an international career that included years living outside Brazil. "Feira de Mangaio" was born from an idea his wife, Glorinha Gadelha, had during an English class in New York — she kept working it out mentally on the subway, until she finished the lyrics sitting in a diner in the city.
🧺 What is a "feira livre"?
The feira livre (open-air market) is a tradition in practically every Brazilian city: stalls set up outdoors, usually once a week, selling everything from fruit and food to clothes, crafts, and regional products. In the Northeast, the feira is also a social gathering spot — with music, traditional food, and neighbors catching up, exactly as the song describes.

Grammar

"Tem" — existence and location

In the song, the second part describes the market using "tem" over and over — the colloquial way to say that something exists or is present in a place (equivalent to "há," which is more formal and barely used in everyday speech).
StructureExample
Tem + noun + placeTem um sanfoneiro no canto da rua. (There's an accordion player on the corner.)
Tem + noun + gerundTem uma senhora fazendo renda. (There's a lady making lace.)

Important: in this sense, "tem" doesn't change in the plural — it's always "tem," even if there's more than one thing (unlike a regular verb like "ter," which would agree with the subject). Compare:
Tem uma banca de frutas ali.(one thing)
Tem várias bancas de frutas ali.(several things — "tem" stays the same)
