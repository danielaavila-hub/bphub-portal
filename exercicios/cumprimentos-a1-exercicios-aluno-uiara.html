Language Reference

Read these before your lesson — you'll use them throughout the activity

📌 Tudo bem? / Tudo bom?
Both mean "how are you?" — used interchangeably as a greeting.
Tudo! E você?All good! And you?
Tudo bem! / Tudo bom! E você?I'm well! And you?
📌 Apresentando-se / Introducing yourself
Sou o José. / Sou a Ana.I'm José. / I'm Ana.
Meu nome é Mariana. E o seu?My name is Mariana. And yours?
Qual é o seu nome? — Muito prazer.What's your name? — Nice to meet you.
📌 Saudações pelo horário / Greetings by time of day
Bom dia!morning, until around noon
Boa tarde!afternoon, until nightfall
Boa noite!evening/night — also used as "goodnight"

📌 Outras expressões / Other expressions
Com licença.Excuse me. (to pass by / step away)
Desculpe.I'm sorry.
Por favor. / Obrigado. / Obrigada. / De nada.Please. / Thanks (m). / Thanks (f). / You're welcome.
Obrigado x obrigada. This word agrees with the gender of the speaker, not the listener: a man always says obrigado, a woman always says obrigada — no matter who they're thanking. You'll also hear very informal greetings like E aí?, Como vai? and Beleza? among friends and younger people — they all work as casual ways to say "how's it going?"
Dica de pronúncia — a letra M. At the end of a syllable (bom, bem, tem, também, trem, tom, som), the M doesn't close your lips the way it does at the start of a word (Maria). It just nasalizes the vowel before it. Practice with your teacher.
